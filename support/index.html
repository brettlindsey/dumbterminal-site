Support

Questions or trouble connecting? Email support [at] dumbterminal [dot] app.

Set up your Mac

On the Mac, open System Settings, then General, then Sharing, and turn on Screen Sharing. The same steps are in the app under Settings, Set Up Your Mac.

A Mac mini with no monitor

Screen Sharing works on a Mac with no display attached, so a Mac mini on a shelf is fine. With no display, macOS chooses the screen size itself, and it may be smaller than you'd like. While connected, open System Settings, then Displays, on the Mac to pick another size. If you want a fixed size every time, a small HDMI "dummy" plug (sold as a headless display adapter) makes the Mac think a monitor is attached, and you choose its resolution in Displays.

No Wi-Fi? Use Personal Hotspot

Turn on Personal Hotspot on your iPhone, and on the Mac join the iPhone's network from the Wi-Fi menu. The Mac and iPhone are then on the same network. If the Mac doesn't appear in Dumb Terminal, add it with + by its address, which the Mac shows in System Settings, Wi-Fi, Details (it usually starts with 172.20.10). This counts as your own network, so Away Access isn't needed. An iPad can join an iPhone's hotspot the same way.

Dumb Terminal can't find or connect to my Mac

  • Check that the iPhone or iPad and the Mac are on the same Wi-Fi network.
  • Allow Dumb Terminal to use the local network in iOS Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Local Network.
  • If a connection hangs, turn Screen Sharing off and on again on the Mac.
  • Away from home, see Reach your Mac from anywhere below.

Reach your Mac from anywhere

Dumb Terminal reaches your Mac away from home over Tailscale, a free app that links your own devices privately.

  1. In Dumb Terminal, open Settings, then Set Up Away From Home. It walks you through the rest.
  2. Get the free Tailscale app on your iPhone or iPad, sign in, and turn it on.
  3. Get Tailscale on your Mac (Dumb Terminal can send you the link) and sign in with the same account.
  4. At home, open each Mac in Dumb Terminal once. Dumb Terminal finds its Tailscale address by itself: there's nothing to type.

Away from home, turn on Tailscale and tap your Mac. The first time, Dumb Terminal offers a free 7-day trial of Away Access. A Mac Dumb Terminal can't find by itself can still be added with + by its Tailscale name or 100.x.y.z address.

Macs on your own network are always free. Away Access comes with Root Access, an optional one-time in-app purchase after the free week, with the price shown in the App Store: no subscription, no account. Restore Purchases is in Dumb Terminal under Settings, Away From Home. In TestFlight beta builds Away Access is included.

If a Mac away from home won't connect, check that Tailscale is connected on both devices (iOS runs one VPN at a time, so turn off any other VPN), and that the Mac is awake with Screen Sharing on.

Tips for away from home:

  • A sleeping Mac can't be reached over Tailscale. On the Mac, turn on Wake for network access in System Settings, Energy (on a laptop: Battery, then Options), or set it never to sleep while plugged in.
  • Keep Tailscale running on the Mac and set it to open at login, so it is back after a restart.
  • Turn Tailscale on in its app on your iPhone or iPad before you tap your Mac. Dumb Terminal reminds you if it's off.
  • Wake-on-LAN only works on your own network, so wake the Mac before you leave if it tends to sleep.

Your Macs on all your devices

Signed in to iCloud, the Macs you save in Dumb Terminal show up on your other iPhones and iPads too, through your own private iCloud. Passwords stay on the device where you saved them, so each device asks once.

Type, click and use shortcuts

Swipe up the small handle at the bottom of a session to bring up the keyboard. Tap the handle for the menu bar: the keyboard, shortcuts such as Screenshot and Force Quit, displays, and Disconnect. Above the keyboard, tap ⌘ to see Copy, Paste, Undo and other shortcuts by name. On iPhone, clicks and drags tap back lightly, like a MacBook trackpad; turn this off with Haptic Clicks in Dumb Terminal's Settings.

Mac gestures work too: swipe three fingers left or right to switch Spaces, up for Mission Control and down for App Exposé, and four fingers left or right to switch apps. On iPad, turn off iPad's own four-finger gestures (Settings, Multitasking & Gestures) to use four fingers in Dumb Terminal.

Tap ⚡ in the row above the keyboard for one-tap Mac actions: Spotlight, Mission Control, App Exposé, Undo and Redo, plus shortcuts of your own. Add, delete and reorder yours in Dumb Terminal's Settings, Shortcut Keys; they are kept on your device.

On iPad, Apple Pencil points exactly where it touches: hover to move the Mac's pointer, tap to click, and draw to drag. A mouse, trackpad or keyboard connected to the iPad works as it would on a Mac, apart from shortcuts iPadOS keeps for itself, such as ⌘Tab and ⌘Space (use the ⚡ shortcuts or a four-finger swipe for those).

To dictate, tap the microphone above the keyboard and speak. Your words appear as you talk; tap Send to type them into the Mac app in front (turn on ↩ to press Return after). Speech is recognized on your device and never sent anywhere.

Use a TV or monitor

Connect your iPhone or iPad to a display by cable or AirPlay while a session is open: your Mac's desktop fills the big screen and your device becomes the trackpad, with the keyboard a swipe away. Unplug it and the desktop comes back to your device.

Sitting at the Mac already? Choose Trackpad Only from the session menu to use your device as a trackpad and keyboard without the picture. It saves battery, and the Mac sends a lighter picture until you show it again.

Lock and unlock your Mac

Tap the handle at the bottom of a session, then the lock. Lock Mac locks the screen. Unlock Mac types the password you connected with into the lock screen; it's there when you connected with your Mac's user name and password. With the keyboard up, Password above it does the same. Your Mac password stays in your iPhone or iPad's Keychain, encrypted by iOS, and is never synced or sent anywhere.

If your Mac was locked when you connected, macOS locks it again when you disconnect, so it is never left open after you put your phone down.

Beta feedback

Testing a beta from TestFlight? Thank you. Send feedback from the TestFlight app: open Dumb Terminal there and tap Send Beta Feedback, or take a screenshot while using Dumb Terminal and tap Share Beta Feedback. You can also email support [at] dumbterminal [dot] app. The build number is in Dumb Terminal's Settings, at the bottom.
